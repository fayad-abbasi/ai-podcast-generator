## Title

Product Manager, Developer Experience. Internal-facing: the customers are the
company's own engineers and engineering leadership, not external users.

## Employer

A mid-size B2B SaaS company. Not named here.

## Manager

A director-level product leader. Not named here. Practises Radical Candor; teaches
by handing over a problem and watching how it gets framed rather than prescribing
the answer. Persuaded by data, not by enthusiasm.

## Time guardrails

~50 hours/week, held deliberately. Deep work happens in an early-morning block
(~2:00 AM) and that time is protected. **Action items must fit inside a normal
workday — never assume the deep-work block is available for them.**

## Methodology

- **Shape Up** — appetite-driven scoping, six-week cycles. The native language.
- **DORA / DX Core 4** — developer-productivity measurement; the vocabulary
  engineering leadership already accepts.
- **Team Topologies** — platform-as-a-product, stream-aligned vs platform teams.
- **Ravi Mehta's PM competency framework** — how scope and growth get discussed.

Treats a platform as a product with adoption, not a service with a ticket queue.
Insists on a measured baseline before anyone claims an improvement.

## Tooling stack

GitHub, Jira, Figma, Miro, an internal developer portal, a developer-experience
measurement platform, and multiple AI coding assistants running in production
across the engineering organisation.

## Working style

- Former equity-research analyst turned PM — reads primary sources, distrusts
  vendor claims, and goes and finds the underlying number.
- Maps systems visually before proposing changes.
- Writes the spec first, then ships in small vertical slices.
- Prefers evidence over opinion, and naming a tradeoff out loud over smoothing it.
- Building a public body of work on developer experience, so a good action item
  can be an observation worth publishing, not only an internal task.
